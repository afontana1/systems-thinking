# EN.645.783 — Systems Engineering Process Improvement

**Credits:** 3

Through lectures and facilitated teamwork, this course presents the fundamental concepts of continuous process improvement in the context of systems engineering. Students will explore how to define, map, model and simulate, assess, manage, and improve a systems engineering process. This will enable students to lead or contribute to a systems engineering process improvement effort on the job, and to be better prepared for certifying their systems engineering expertise.

### How to use this plan

Use one **real or fictional systems engineering process** all semester. Good choices:

* requirements development and change control
* architecture review and baseline management
* verification and validation planning
* risk management and technical review workflow
* integration/readiness management

Plan on about **8–10 hours per week**:

* 3 hours reading
* 2 hours lectures/videos/reference study
* 2–3 hours process mapping/modeling
* 1–2 hours write-up and reflection

By the end, you should have a small **process-improvement portfolio**:

* process charter
* as-is process map
* metrics baseline
* assessment findings
* to-be process design
* pilot plan
* control/monitoring plan
* certification-oriented study notes

### Week 1 — What process improvement means in systems engineering

**Goal**
Understand why systems engineering process improvement is about better outcomes, not just more documentation.

**Read**

* SEBoK: **Systems Engineering and Management** for how engineering processes support strategic and project objectives. ([SEBoK][184])
* SEBoK: **Systems Engineering Overview** for the baseline definition of systems engineering across the full lifecycle. ([SEBoK][185])
* ASQ overview of **continuous improvement**, including PDCA, Lean, Six Sigma, and TQM. ([ASQ][186])

**Exercises**

1. Write a 1-page memo: “What makes a systems engineering process effective?”
2. Choose one process to improve.
3. Define its customers, suppliers, inputs, outputs, and pain points.

**Deliverable**

* Process selection memo
* SIPOC-style high-level process view

---

### Week 2 — Process frameworks and standards

**Goal**
Learn the reference models that improvement efforts are usually compared against.

**Read**

* INCOSE standards page listing **ISO/IEC/IEEE 15288:2023** and related lifecycle standards. ([INCOSE][187])
* ISO page for **ISO/IEC/IEEE 15288**, which describes it as a common framework of lifecycle process descriptions. ([ISO][188])
* SEBoK: **Systems Engineering Standards**. ([SEBoK][189])
* CMMI Institute overview of **CMMI V3.0**. ([CMMI Institute][190])

**Exercises**

1. Map your chosen process to the relevant 15288 process areas.
2. Note where your process is:

   * missing
   * informal
   * inconsistent
   * over-controlled
3. Write a 2-page comparison: ISO-style lifecycle process thinking vs CMMI-style capability improvement.

**Deliverable**

* Standards crosswalk
* Framework comparison note

---

### Week 3 — Defining and scoping the process

**Goal**
Set clear boundaries before trying to improve anything.

**Read**

* ASQ documentation and process mapping guide, especially the role of clear documentation and mapping in improvement. ([ASQ][191])
* ASQ process mapping article. ([ASQ][192])

**Exercises**

1. Write a process charter with:

   * problem statement
   * scope
   * objectives
   * stakeholders
   * constraints
   * expected benefits
2. Define start and end points for the process.
3. List the events that trigger the process and the artifacts it produces.

**Deliverable**

* Process charter
* Scope and boundary definition

---

### Week 4 — Mapping the current process

**Goal**
Create an accurate **as-is** view before designing improvements.

**Read**

* ASQ mapping guidance again. ([ASQ][191])
* SEBoK: **Technical Management Processes** to anchor mapping in real SE work such as planning, risk, configuration, measurement, and quality assurance. ([SEBoK][49])

**Exercises**

1. Build an as-is process map using flowchart, swimlane, or BPMN-style notation.
2. Capture:

   * roles
   * handoffs
   * approvals
   * rework loops
   * waiting time
3. Mark known pain points in the flow.

**Deliverable**

* As-is process map v1

---

### Week 5 — Measuring the process

**Goal**
Define how you will know whether the process is improving.

**Read**

* SEBoK: **Measurement**. ([SEBoK][193])
* SEBoK: **Systems Engineering Measurement Primer** and **Technical Measurement Guide** references. ([SEBoK][194])
* SEBoK: **Quality Management** for the relationship between quality and process discipline. ([SEBoK][195])

**Exercises**

1. Define 8–10 metrics for your process, such as:

   * cycle time
   * touch time
   * review turnaround
   * defect escape rate
   * rework percentage
   * requirements churn
   * risk closure rate
   * first-pass approval rate
2. For each metric, specify:

   * definition
   * source
   * collection method
   * update frequency
   * decision supported
3. Create a baseline dashboard sketch.

**Deliverable**

* Process metrics catalog
* Baseline dashboard

---

### Week 6 — Assessing the current state

**Goal**
Evaluate the process systematically rather than anecdotally.

**Read**

* CMMI overview and maturity-level explanation. ([CMMI Institute][196])
* SEBoK: **Systems Engineering and Management** for adapting processes to organizational and project needs. ([SEBoK][184])

**Exercises**

1. Assess your process against criteria like:

   * defined
   * repeatable
   * measurable
   * governed
   * tailored
   * continuously improved
2. Score the process using a simple maturity rubric.
3. Identify top 5 causes of poor performance.

**Deliverable**

* Current-state assessment report
* Gap analysis

---

### Week 7 — Modeling and simulation for process improvement

**Goal**
Use lightweight modeling to test improvement ideas before rollout.

**Read**

* NIST note on systems engineering model-to-simulation translation, useful for thinking about simulation as a decision aid. ([NIST][197])
* NIST process-improvement/DOE aid for thinking experimentally about process variables and response variables. ([NIST][119])

**Exercises**

1. Build a simple process simulation model in spreadsheet, discrete-event tool, or system-dynamics style.
2. Model at least:

   * arrival rate
   * processing time
   * queue/backlog
   * rework probability
3. Test 3 scenarios:

   * more staffing
   * fewer approvals
   * earlier defect detection
4. Compare outcomes on cycle time and quality.

**Deliverable**

* Simple process model
* Scenario comparison memo

---

### Week 8 — Designing the improved process

**Goal**
Create a realistic **to-be** process, not just a list of complaints.

**Read**

* ASQ **DMAIC** overview for a structured improvement pattern. ([ASQ][198])
* ASQ continuous improvement resources for PDCA/Lean/Six Sigma positioning. ([ASQ][186])

**Exercises**

1. Redesign the process using one improvement method:

   * PDCA
   * DMAIC
   * Lean
   * hybrid approach
2. Remove or reduce:

   * redundant approvals
   * unclear ownership
   * rework loops
   * manual handoffs
3. Define the future-state roles and controls.

**Deliverable**

* To-be process map
* Improvement rationale memo

---

### Week 9 — Managing change and implementation

**Goal**
Learn how to introduce a better process without causing organizational backlash.

**Read**

* SEBoK: **Systems Engineering and Project Management** for how technical work interacts with planning, staffing, and execution constraints. ([SEBoK][199])
* SEBoK: **Quality Management** for institutionalizing improved practices. ([SEBoK][195])

**Exercises**

1. Create an implementation plan covering:

   * pilot scope
   * training
   * tool changes
   * governance updates
   * communication plan
2. Identify adoption risks.
3. Define who owns the process after launch.

**Deliverable**

* Implementation roadmap
* Adoption risk register

---

### Week 10 — Control, sustainment, and continuous improvement

**Goal**
Make improvement stick.

**Read**

* ASQ continuous improvement and DMAIC resources. ([ASQ][186])
* SEBoK measurement resources again, focusing on ongoing monitoring. ([SEBoK][193])

**Exercises**

1. Build a control plan:

   * control metrics
   * review cadence
   * escalation rules
   * audit/check methods
2. Define thresholds for intervention.
3. Write a short “how we will continuously improve this process” note.

**Deliverable**

* Process control plan
* Monitoring dashboard v2

---

### Week 11 — Certification-oriented systems engineering review

**Goal**
Connect process-improvement knowledge to systems engineering certification preparation.

INCOSE currently offers **ASEP, CSEP, and ESEP** certification levels, and the March 2026 program-definition document confirms that three-level structure. ([INCOSE][200])

**Read**

* INCOSE certification overview. ([INCOSE][200])
* INCOSE “Start Your Certification” and ASEP pages. ([INCOSE][201])

**Exercises**

1. Build a study sheet for:

   * lifecycle processes
   * technical management processes
   * measurement
   * quality
   * risk
   * configuration
   * verification/validation
2. Write 20 practice short-answer questions from your weekly material.
3. Map your portfolio artifacts to likely certification knowledge areas.

**Deliverable**

* Certification prep notes
* Self-quiz bank

---

### Week 12 — Final process improvement package

**Goal**
Synthesize the full effort into something you could present at work.

**Exercises**
Assemble a final package with:

1. process charter
2. as-is process map
3. metrics baseline
4. assessment/gap analysis
5. simulation or scenario analysis
6. to-be process design
7. implementation roadmap
8. control plan
9. certification prep summary

Write a **4–6 page summary memo** explaining:

* what process was improved
* how you assessed it
* what evidence justified the redesign
* what implementation risks remain
* how success will be monitored over time

**Deliverable**

* Final process-improvement binder
* Executive summary memo

### Best resource stack

These are the strongest anchors for the full 12 weeks:

* **SEBoK v2.13**, especially systems engineering management, technical management processes, measurement, and quality management. ([SEBoK][183])
* **ISO/IEC/IEEE 15288:2023** as the core lifecycle process framework referenced by INCOSE. ([INCOSE][187])
* **CMMI V3.0** for structured capability improvement and maturity thinking. ([CMMI Institute][190])
* **ASQ continuous improvement / DMAIC / process mapping** resources for practical improvement tools. ([ASQ][186])
* **INCOSE SEP certification** pages if you want to tie the course directly to certification preparation. ([INCOSE][200])

### What you should be able to do after 12 weeks

By the end, you should be able to:

* define and scope a systems engineering process
* map the current process clearly
* choose useful process metrics
* assess process maturity and gaps
* model or simulate improvement options
* design a realistic future-state process
* plan rollout and sustainment
* connect process-improvement work to INCOSE-style certification prep
