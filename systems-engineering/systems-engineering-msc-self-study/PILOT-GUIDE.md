# Pilot and implementation guide

[Back to program README](README.md) · [Read the program audit](PROGRAM-AUDIT.md)

## 1. Purpose

The first implementation should test whether the curriculum is teachable, assessable, reproducible, and sustainable—not merely whether the documents are extensive. A pilot should produce evidence about learner readiness, actual workload, resource quality, tool friction, exercise clarity, rubric usability, and the value of required revision.

## 2. Pilot modes

### Solo pilot

Use when no mentor or cohort is available.

- Record all work in version control.
- Record review presentations and oral defenses.
- Separate the initial submission from the self-review by at least 24 hours.
- Use role-based red teams: sponsor, operator, maintainer, safety, cybersecurity, supplier, tester, and affected public.
- Do not view answer guidance until an initial attempt is baselined.

### Mentored pilot

Preferred for advanced and capstone courses.

- A mentor reviews readiness, major baselines, critical failures, and final defenses.
- The mentor should challenge assumptions and evidence rather than redesign the artifact for the learner.
- Use a finding log with severity, owner, due date, disposition, and closure evidence.

### Cohort pilot

Preferred for courses involving negotiation, enterprise behavior, process improvement, or contested problem framing.

- Assign rotating review and stakeholder roles.
- Require individual artifacts in addition to collaborative work.
- Record contribution, dissent, decisions, and unresolved disagreement.
- Use peer feedback as evidence, not as a substitute for technical scoring.

## 3. Recommended pilot sequence

Do not pilot all 20 courses before making improvements. Use a representative sequence:

1. **EN.645.662** — tests the common weekly pattern and foundation assumptions.
2. **EN.645.631** — tests modeling-tool paths, repository queries, and model review.
3. **EN.645.767** — tests open-ended concept work, quantitative trades, and formal review.
4. **EN.645.757** — tests code, data, simulation, reproducibility, and credibility.
5. **EN.645.782** — tests digital-thread/twin and mission integration.
6. **EN.645.771 or EN.645.742** — tests distributed authority, complexity, critique, and advanced oral defense.

After these pilots, revise the common templates and propagate only changes that are supported by evidence.

## 4. Pilot entry record

Before each course, record:

- prior courses or equivalent experience;
- diagnostic score and remediations;
- available hours per week;
- tool and operating-system configuration;
- access to required and recommended resources;
- mentor or review support;
- expected interruptions and schedule risks;
- learning objective and intended professional application.

## 5. Weekly implementation record

For every week, record:

| Measure | Record |
|---|---|
| Planned and actual hours | Total and by activity |
| Readiness result | First attempt, remediation, retest |
| Resource usability | Clear, too broad, inaccessible, obsolete, or redundant |
| Tool friction | Installation, license, data, model, package, or performance issue |
| Exercise clarity | Missing input, ambiguous constraint, unclear output, or well specified |
| First-submission score | Overall and critical criteria |
| Revision effort | Findings, hours, and closure evidence |
| Knowledge-check result | First and final attempt |
| Confidence | 1–5, with explanation |
| Transfer | How the week affected a later artifact or professional task |

## 6. Calibration criteria

Flag a week for revision when any condition occurs:

- median actual workload differs from the target by more than 20%;
- prepared learners cannot begin because an input, dataset, or tool instruction is missing;
- more than one-third of learners interpret the deliverable differently;
- rubric criteria cannot be scored consistently by two reviewers;
- a critical failure is common because the teaching sequence did not prepare the learner;
- the knowledge check measures terminology but not the week’s stated judgment or method;
- a worked example cannot be reproduced from its source and assumptions;
- a required external source is inaccessible with no substitute;
- the learner can pass without producing the evidence needed by the next course.

## 7. Review discipline

Each formal review should include:

1. decision requested;
2. entry criteria and submitted baseline;
3. assumptions, exclusions, limitations, and non-claims;
4. traceability from need or decision to evidence;
5. independent checks and negative evidence;
6. findings by severity;
7. decision, conditions, actions, and accepted residual risk;
8. disposition and configuration update.

A presentation is not a review unless it produces a controlled decision and finding record.

## 8. Scoring and mastery

- Use the published rubric before beginning the assignment.
- Preserve the first submission and score separately from the revised submission.
- Do not average away a critical failure.
- Require the learner to explain the change made and why it resolves the finding.
- Use oral questions to test authorship, reasoning, and transfer—not memory of wording.
- Require a clean reproduction or live change for code, models, analytics, and digital-thread work.

## 9. External review plan

For Version 1.1, seek at least:

- one practicing systems engineer for Phases 0–2;
- one modeling/statistics specialist for Phase 3;
- one digital/agile/process practitioner for Phase 4;
- one SoS, enterprise, or complexity practitioner for Phase 5;
- one reviewer focused on safety, cybersecurity, human factors/accessibility, and public-interest issues across phases.

Ask reviewers to identify unsupported claims, missing prerequisites, unrealistic work products, weak evidence, hidden tool assumptions, and professional practices that are misrepresented or absent.

## 10. Course closeout

At the end of each pilot course, produce:

- actual workload distribution;
- scores before and after revision;
- unresolved resource and tool issues;
- unclear or redundant material;
- missing solution assets;
- prerequisite gaps;
- portfolio-handoff defects;
- proposed changes with priority and rationale;
- a decision: retain, revise, split, merge activities, or suspend pending evidence.

Do not rewrite material solely because one learner preferred another style. Prioritize repeated evidence, safety or integrity concerns, inaccessible resources, and defects that block mastery or handoff.

## 11. Program completion record

A learner claiming completion should retain:

- the final controlled repository;
- course and phase review records;
- first and revised submissions for major artifacts;
- scores and critical-failure closures;
- oral-defense recordings or notes;
- actual time records;
- competency evidence index;
- final professional-development reflection and limitations statement.
